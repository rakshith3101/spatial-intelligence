Aerospace engineering applies principles of physics to design aircraft and spacecraft.
Aerodynamic design reduces drag and increases fuel efficiency in flight.
Rocket propulsion relies on Newton's third law of motion.
Structural materials in aircraft must balance strength with minimal weight.
Combustion in jet engines generates thrust through controlled temperature and pressure.
Satellite orbits are maintained by gravitational balance and velocity.
Wind tunnels test aerodynamic designs before physical construction.
Control systems manage aircraft stability through continuous feedback loops.
Thermal management protects spacecraft from extreme temperature variations in space.
Navigation systems use triangulation from celestial bodies and ground stations.
Autonomous systems allow aircraft to respond to changing environmental conditions.
Materials science develops alloys that withstand extreme stress and temperature.
Fluid dynamics governs air flow around wings and fuselage.
Spacecraft reentry requires precise calculations to survive atmospheric friction.
Guidance systems use algorithms to adjust trajectory during flight.
Human factors engineering ensures pilot controls are intuitive and safe.
Power systems in aircraft convert fuel or electricity into mechanical energy.
Communication systems maintain reliable contact across vast distances.
Stress analysis predicts how structures respond to forces during operation.
Telemetry systems collect and transmit data about vehicle performance.
